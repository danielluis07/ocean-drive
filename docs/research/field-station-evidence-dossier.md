# Field Station evidence dossier

Research note for the Wayfinder ticket **Assemble the Field Station evidence dossier**. Prepared 2026-09-05.

## Decision context

The governing decisions are [Choose the scientific story and evidence boundaries](https://github.com/danielluis07/ocean-drive/issues/5#issuecomment-5552170623) and [Define the Expedition narrative and Field Station structure](https://github.com/danielluis07/ocean-drive/issues/7#issuecomment-5553593852).

The Expedition is a retrospective composite journey through evidence from the documented 2019 Abrolhos marine heatwave. The scientific sequence is:

1. **Pulso de Calor** — what happened;
2. **Corais sob Estresse** — what bleaching means, including that it is not automatic death;
3. **Respostas Desiguais** — how outcomes varied among organisms and places;
4. **Convergência** — synthesis only, with no new evidence.

The fictional institute, vessel, crew, station names, and itinerary may be invented. Geography, dates, organisms, observations, measurements, and scientific relationships must remain factual and traceable. The interface must identify the journey as a retrospective composite and must not imply that its fictional vessel collected these data.

## Recommended evidence spine

Use three records, with distinct roles:

1. **Duarte et al. (2020)** is the principal event paper. It supplies the heat-exposure record and a spatial snapshot of coral condition at Coroa Vermelha, Pedra do Silva, and Virada de Fora. It is the source for Pulso de Calor and the three-site comparison in Respostas Desiguais. [Peer-reviewed article](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2020.00179); [underlying transect images](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3713345).
2. **ICMBio, Abrolhos Marine National Park (2021)** is the first-party monitoring report. It supplies the event chronology, monthly community monitoring, a seven-site *Millepora alcicornis* mortality census, and weekly photographs of twelve fixed colonies. It is the most concrete local record for explaining the difference between bleaching, recovery, and death. [Official report (PDF)](https://www.gov.br/icmbio/pt-br/assuntos/biodiversidade/unidade-de-conservacao/unidades-de-biomas/marinho/lista-de-ucs/parna-marinho-dos-abrolhos/pesquisa-e-monitoramento/monitoramento_recifal/relatorio_monitoramento_recifal_2019_20.pdf).
3. **Ferreira et al. (2021)** is the peer-reviewed account of the National Park monitoring. Its abstract reports contrasting trajectories for the branching hydrocoral *M. alcicornis* and the massive endemic coral *Mussismilia braziliensis*. Use it to support the high-level contrast, while using the ICMBio report for method and sample-size caveats. [Peer-reviewed article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00227-021-03863-6).
4. **Corazza et al. (2024)** supplies a longer-term counterpoint from three southern Bahia reefs. It separates recovery of color/health among surviving colonies from recovery of coral cover after mortality. Use it as a caveat, not as a fourth Field Station storyline. [Peer-reviewed article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00227-024-04432-3).

For explanatory context only, NOAA is an authoritative source for the bleaching mechanism and its Coral Reef Watch metric:

- [NOAA Ocean Service: What is coral bleaching?](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/coral_bleach.html)
- [NOAA Coral Reef Watch: Degree Heating Week product](https://coralreefwatch.noaa.gov/product/5km/index_5km_dhw.php)

## Geographic and temporal frame

### The event is regional; the observations are site-specific

Duarte et al. studied three reefs in the Abrolhos Bank region:

| Site | Reported position and setting | Sampling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coroa Vermelha | 16°20′41″ S, 38°58′25″ W; about 3 km offshore at Santa Cruz Cabrália, outside the protected area | 9 video transects |
| Pedra do Silva | 16°53′16″ S, 39°05′30″ W; Itacolomis Reef Complex, inside the Corumbau Marine Extractive Reserve | 11 video transects |
| Virada de Fora | 16°54′02″ S, 39°02′28″ W; Itacolomis Reef Complex, inside the Corumbau Marine Extractive Reserve | 10 video transects |

Pedra do Silva and Virada de Fora are about 6 km apart; Coroa Vermelha and the Itacolomis Reef Complex are about 70 km apart north–south. Surveys ran from 25 June through 5 July 2019, at 4–8 m depth. Each 20 m video transect was analyzed with roughly 2,000 random points. ([Duarte et al., Methods](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2020.00179/full#h5))

The ICMBio record concerns seven sites inside Abrolhos Marine National Park: Mato Verde, Sueste Norte, Língua da Siriba, Portinho Norte, Chapeirão Jean Pierre, Chapeirão Faca Cega, and Chapeirão Débora. Semiannual surveys took place in April 2019, August 2019, and January 2020; targeted bleaching protocols added monthly monitoring at Mato Verde and Jean Pierre from February through August 2019. Six fixed colonies of each focal species were photographed weekly at Mato Verde from February through August. ([ICMBio report, pp. 5, 9–11, 30–36](https://www.gov.br/icmbio/pt-br/assuntos/biodiversidade/unidade-de-conservacao/unidades-de-biomas/marinho/lista-de-ucs/parna-marinho-dos-abrolhos/pesquisa-e-monitoramento/monitoramento_recifal/relatorio_monitoramento_recifal_2019_20.pdf#page=10))

These are complementary datasets, not repeated measurements of the same colonies or reefs. Label the place and method whenever a number is shown.

### Event chronology that the sources support

- NOAA satellite-derived SST at the Abrolhos virtual station passed 28°C on 29 January 2019, peaked at 29.3°C on 23 February, and remained above 28°C until 11 May, according to the ICMBio report. ([ICMBio report, p. 30](https://www.gov.br/icmbio/pt-br/assuntos/biodiversidade/unidade-de-conservacao/unidades-de-biomas/marinho/lista-de-ucs/parna-marinho-dos-abrolhos/pesquisa-e-monitoramento/monitoramento_recifal/relatorio_monitoramento_recifal_2019_20.pdf#page=31))
- The published Abrolhos Coral Reef Watch series reached 19.65 degree heating weeks (DHW) on 10 May 2019. DHW remained above 15 for 50 consecutive days, 12 April–31 May. ([Duarte et al., Results](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2020.00179/full#h8))
- Park observers first noted partially bleached *M. alcicornis* at Mato Verde in February. Bleaching peaked in April–May; cooling began in the second half of May; recovery and the first recently dead colonies were observed from June onward. ([ICMBio report, pp. 30–33](https://www.gov.br/icmbio/pt-br/assuntos/biodiversidade/unidade-de-conservacao/unidades-de-biomas/marinho/lista-de-ucs/parna-marinho-dos-abrolhos/pesquisa-e-monitoramento/monitoramento_recifal/relatorio_monitoramento_recifal_2019_20.pdf#page=31))
- Duarte et al.'s three-site condition survey occurred later, from 25 June to 5 July, so its high “recently dead” proportions are a post-peak snapshot rather than measurements taken at the thermal maximum. ([Duarte et al., Methods](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2020.00179/full#h5))

## Metric and language guardrails

### Degree heating weeks

DHW is accumulated thermal stress over the most recent 12 weeks, expressed in °C-weeks. Conceptually, one DHW can represent one week at 1°C above the local maximum-monthly-mean threshold. It is an exposure index derived from satellite SST, not a thermometer reading at a coral colony and not a percentage. ([NOAA Coral Reef Watch](https://coralreefwatch.noaa.gov/product/5km/index_5km_dhw.php); [Duarte et al., Methods](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2020.00179/full#h7))

Use `19.65 °C-weeks` or `19,65 °C-semanas` in precise copy. Do not write `19.65°C`, `19.65 weeks`, or turn the value into fictional buoy telemetry.

The Duarte analysis names NOAA CoralTemp Version 1.0 at nominal 5 km resolution and uses a 2010–July 2019 comparison series. NOAA states that CoralTemp v1.0 was renamed v3.1 on 21 March 2019 with no change to the product, but its alert-level scheme was expanded in December 2023. Cite `19.65 DHW` as the historical value reported in the 2020 paper; do not retroactively call the 2019 event a later operational alert level. Duarte et al. also acknowledge that local temperature loggers would have offered higher-resolution comparisons among sites; their heat record is regional satellite SST. ([NOAA methodology](https://coralreefwatch.noaa.gov/product/5km/methodology.php); [Duarte et al., Methods and Discussion](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2020.00179/full#h7))

### Bleaching, recovery, and mortality

Bleaching is the visible result of stress disrupting the relationship between the animal and its symbiotic algae; the coral becomes pale or white after losing algae and/or their pigments. A bleached colony is alive but stressed. It may recover if stress subsides, or die if severe stress persists. Heat is a major cause, but bleaching can also have other environmental causes. ([NOAA Ocean Service](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/coral_bleach.html))

For its image classification, Duarte et al. defined:

- healthy: no bleaching signal;
- partially bleached: pigmentation at least 50% of resistant colonies;
- severely bleached: pigmentation below 50%;
- recently dead: recent secondary algae on exposed skeleton and/or tissue loss above 90%;
- old dead: skeleton colonized by established turf, crustose coralline algae, or macroalgae.

Thus, the paper's “dead (%)” values are survey classifications of colony condition/cover, not a before-and-after count of individually tracked deaths. ([Duarte et al., Methods](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2020.00179/full#h6))

## Pulso de Calor

### Defensible claim set

| Claim | Exact support | Required qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Abrolhos experienced exceptional accumulated heat stress in 2019. | DHW peaked at 19.65 on 10 May; the paper calls this the Abrolhos regional record in its analyzed series. [Duarte et al.](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2020.00179) | Attribute the record to the published NOAA Abrolhos station series; do not imply an in-situ sensor at every Field Station. |
| The exposure was sustained, not a one-day spike. | DHW exceeded 15 for 50 consecutive days, 12 April–31 May; it exceeded 10 on 102 days during the January–July 2019 analysis. [Duarte et al., Results](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2020.00179/full#h8) | The 50-day statement is about an accumulated-stress index remaining above 15, not 50 days at 15°C above normal. |
| Surface water crossed the local heat threshold before visible impacts unfolded. | ICMBio reports SST above 28°C from 29 January to 11 May, a 29.3°C peak on 23 February, and initial *M. alcicornis* bleaching in February. [ICMBio report, p. 30](https://www.gov.br/icmbio/pt-br/assuntos/biodiversidade/unidade-de-conservacao/unidades-de-biomas/marinho/lista-de-ucs/parna-marinho-dos-abrolhos/pesquisa-e-monitoramento/monitoramento_recifal/relatorio_monitoramento_recifal_2019_20.pdf#page=31) | The report uses a rounded 28°C “critical” line while Duarte et al. report a 27.9°C regional bleaching threshold. Pick one representation and cite it; do not pretend they are two independent thresholds. |
| The biological observations followed the thermal peak. | Targeted Park monitoring covered February–August; the northern three-reef survey was 25 June–5 July. [ICMBio report](https://www.gov.br/icmbio/pt-br/assuntos/biodiversidade/unidade-de-conservacao/unidades-de-biomas/marinho/lista-de-ucs/parna-marinho-dos-abrolhos/pesquisa-e-monitoramento/monitoramento_recifal/relatorio_monitoramento_recifal_2019_20.pdf#page=10); [Duarte et al.](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2020.00179) | Present a historical timeline, not live conditions. |

### Safe Portuguese wording

> Em 10 de maio de 2019, o índice de estresse térmico acumulado na estação de Abrolhos chegou a 19,65 °C-semanas. O valor permaneceu acima de 15 por 50 dias consecutivos. Era um registro regional de calor persistente — não uma medição feita por esta expedição fictícia.

This is supportable if the citation disclosure explains DHW. A shorter passage can say “o maior valor registrado na série analisada para Abrolhos” rather than the unbounded “o maior já registrado”.

### Do not claim

- Do not say climate change **caused this specific 2019 event**. None of the selected sources performs event attribution. It is safe to state only that ocean warming increases the broader risk of marine heatwaves and bleaching, separately sourced.
- Do not describe 19.65 DHW as an in-situ measurement or as a value shared uniformly by all survey sites.
- Do not call the June–July transects observations from the heat peak.
- Do not describe the historical observations as current reef condition.

## Corais sob Estresse

### Defensible claim set

| Claim | Exact support | Required qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bleaching is a stress response, not automatic death. | NOAA explains that stressed corals lose symbiotic algae, become white, and can recover or die depending on severity and duration. [NOAA Ocean Service](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/coral_bleach.html) | Avoid describing all bleaching as heat-caused; use the 2019 local chronology to connect this episode to heat. |
| Recovery followed cooling for many colonies in the Park survey. | At Mato Verde, more than 70% of sampled colonies showed bleaching at the May peak; by August 92.6% were classed healthy. At Jean Pierre, more than 70% were bleached in April–May and about 80% were healthy in August. [ICMBio report, pp. 31–33](https://www.gov.br/icmbio/pt-br/assuntos/biodiversidade/unidade-de-conservacao/unidades-de-biomas/marinho/lista-de-ucs/parna-marinho-dos-abrolhos/pesquisa-e-monitoramento/monitoramento_recifal/relatorio_monitoramento_recifal_2019_20.pdf#page=32) | These monthly percentages describe sampled community condition at each visit; they are not all individually tracked colonies. |
| A locally tracked brain coral demonstrates recovery directly. | Of six fixed *M. braziliensis* colonies, one bleached and recovered after temperature declined; four never bleached; one died. [ICMBio report, pp. 34–36](https://www.gov.br/icmbio/pt-br/assuntos/biodiversidade/unidade-de-conservacao/unidades-de-biomas/marinho/lista-de-ucs/parna-marinho-dos-abrolhos/pesquisa-e-monitoramento/monitoramento_recifal/relatorio_monitoramento_recifal_2019_20.pdf#page=35) | This is a vivid six-colony case series, not a population estimate. The colony that died had already begun bleaching when marked. |
| Bleaching can precede death when stress is severe. | All six fixed *M. alcicornis* colonies bleached completely, then were colonized successively by cyanobacteria, turf algae, and crustose coralline algae. [ICMBio report, pp. 34–35](https://www.gov.br/icmbio/pt-br/assuntos/biodiversidade/unidade-de-conservacao/unidades-de-biomas/marinho/lista-de-ucs/parna-marinho-dos-abrolhos/pesquisa-e-monitoramento/monitoramento_recifal/relatorio_monitoramento_recifal_2019_20.pdf#page=35) | Keep this as a sequence observed in six selected, shallow Mato Verde colonies, not the universal fate of bleached corals. |

### Safe Portuguese wording

> Branquear não é o mesmo que morrer. Sob estresse, o coral perde parte das algas simbióticas que vivem em seus tecidos e sua cor desaparece. Se o estresse diminui, algumas colônias se recuperam; se persiste, outras morrem. Em Abrolhos, os dois caminhos foram observados em 2019.

For a local concrete follow-up:

> Em Mato Verde, uma colônia acompanhada de *Mussismilia braziliensis* branqueou e voltou a ficar saudável quando a água esfriou. Seis colônias acompanhadas de *Millepora alcicornis* tiveram outro destino: branquearam por completo e morreram.

The second passage must disclose the six-colony sample and should not present morphology alone as the proven cause of the difference.

### Do not claim

- Do not say expelled algae always “leave” voluntarily or that the white material is dead tissue. The white appearance exposes the pale skeleton through living, translucent tissue.
- Do not equate pale, partially bleached, severely bleached, recently dead, and old dead categories.
- Do not infer recovery merely from a low late-season bleaching percentage when individually tracked evidence is available.
- Do not make the six fixed colonies representative of every colony or species at Abrolhos.

## Respostas Desiguais

This station should show inequality on two axes: **among taxa at the same sites** and **within the same taxon among places**. It should not reduce the event to a universal “branching loses, massive wins” law.

### Organism-level evidence from the same three-site survey

Percentages below are the share of each taxon's surveyed condition/cover class in late June–early July 2019, as reported in Duarte et al. Table 2.

| Taxon | Coroa Vermelha: bleached / dead | Pedra do Silva: bleached / dead | Virada de Fora: bleached / dead | Supported reading |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| *Millepora alcicornis* (branching fire hydrocoral) | 25.3% / 43.3% | 4.1% / 89.1% | 8.1% / 83.5% | By the post-peak survey, this key reef builder had the highest mortality at every site, but the magnitude varied strongly by site. |
| *Mussismilia braziliensis* (massive endemic coral) | 27.8% / 0% | 35.4% / 0% | 10.2% / 0.2% | Substantial bleaching coexisted with very low observed mortality. |
| *Mussismilia harttii* (phaceloid coral) | 45.3% / 8.2% | 40.5% / 12.9% | 11.5% / 5.2% | It had the highest mortality among the three *Mussismilia* species, yet far less than *M. alcicornis*. |
| *Mussismilia hispida* (massive coral) | 38.9% / 0.6% | 44.0% / 2.5% | 22.0% / 3.9% | High bleaching did not map one-to-one onto mortality. |

Source: [Duarte et al., Table 2 and Figure 3](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2020.00179/full#T2).

Use the reported uncertainty when featuring *M. alcicornis* as a headline comparison: 43.3 ± 12.0% at Coroa Vermelha, 89.1 ± 3.9% at Pedra do Silva, and 83.5 ± 9.0% at Virada de Fora. The article reports these as mortality estimates from independent transects. ([Duarte et al., abstract](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2020.00179))

### Independent National Park evidence

- In August 2019, ICMBio evaluated 30 randomly selected *M. alcicornis* colonies near transects at each of seven sites. Mean colony mortality ranged from 63.8% (SD 38.6) at deeper Faca Cega to 98.7% (SD 5.3) at Sueste Norte; the other five site means were 87.5–96.8%. ([ICMBio report, pp. 33–34](https://www.gov.br/icmbio/pt-br/assuntos/biodiversidade/unidade-de-conservacao/unidades-de-biomas/marinho/lista-de-ucs/parna-marinho-dos-abrolhos/pesquisa-e-monitoramento/monitoramento_recifal/relatorio_monitoramento_recifal_2019_20.pdf#page=34))
- ICMBio suggests greater depth as a possible reason for the lower Faca Cega result (transects 9–13 m), but did not establish depth as the cause. Preserve “possibly” or omit the mechanism. ([ICMBio report, p. 34](https://www.gov.br/icmbio/pt-br/assuntos/biodiversidade/unidade-de-conservacao/unidades-de-biomas/marinho/lista-de-ucs/parna-marinho-dos-abrolhos/pesquisa-e-monitoramento/monitoramento_recifal/relatorio_monitoramento_recifal_2019_20.pdf#page=35))
- The peer-reviewed longitudinal analysis reports bleaching in 100% of monitored *M. alcicornis* and 80% of monitored *M. braziliensis* colonies; about 90% *M. alcicornis* mortality contrasted with recovery to healthy condition in 90% of *M. braziliensis*. ([Ferreira et al., abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00227-021-03863-6))
- In the Park's April seven-site sampling, *M. alcicornis*, *Porites astreoides*, and *Favia gravida* were the first species to bleach severely, while *Montastraea cavernosa*, *M. braziliensis*, and *Agaricia* sp. had lower April bleaching rates. ([ICMBio report, pp. 19–20](https://www.gov.br/icmbio/pt-br/assuntos/biodiversidade/unidade-de-conservacao/unidades-de-biomas/marinho/lista-de-ucs/parna-marinho-dos-abrolhos/pesquisa-e-monitoramento/monitoramento_recifal/relatorio_monitoramento_recifal_2019_20.pdf#page=20))

A later study at three other southern Bahia reefs found no increase in coral cover at assemblage or species level three years after the 2019 event. That does not contradict Ferreira et al.'s recovery-to-healthy-state result: a surviving colony can regain healthy coloration while coral cover already lost to mortality remains unrecovered. The later study also reports a different local heat peak (14.1 °C-weeks), confirming that these are different sites and samples rather than a continuation of the 19.65-DHW virtual-station series. ([Corazza et al., abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00227-024-04432-3))

### Safe Portuguese wording

> O mesmo calor não produziu a mesma resposta. No levantamento de junho e julho, a mortalidade observada de *Millepora alcicornis* variou de 43,3% em Coroa Vermelha a 89,1% em Pedra do Silva. Nos mesmos recifes, *Mussismilia braziliensis* apresentou branqueamento, mas mortalidade entre 0% e 0,2%.

An alternate organism-first passage:

> Duas construtoras do recife atravessaram o mesmo evento por caminhos diferentes. A *Millepora alcicornis*, ramificada, sofreu mortalidade extensa. A *Mussismilia braziliensis*, maciça e endêmica do Brasil, branqueou amplamente, mas a maior parte das colônias monitoradas se recuperou.

Pair either version with place, dates, method, and sample disclosure. Use “associated with” rather than asserting that colony shape alone caused survival or death.

### Do not claim

- Do not generalize from two focal species that all branching corals die or all massive corals recover.
- Do not say 89.1% of the whole reef died. That value is the reported *M. alcicornis* mortality at Pedra do Silva. Total recently dead coral cover was 15.6 ± 3.2% there. ([Duarte et al., Table 3](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2020.00179/full#T3))
- Do not compare raw percentages across Duarte's condition/cover analysis, ICMBio's per-colony percent-mortality census, and Ferreira's longitudinal trajectories as though they were the same metric.
- Do not attribute site differences to protection, fishing, depth, turbidity, genotype, symbionts, or local adaptation without a dedicated analysis. The selected records describe patterns and offer hypotheses; they do not isolate those causes.
- Do not claim that surviving colonies were proven genetically heat-tolerant. Duarte et al. identify this as a research possibility, not a demonstrated mechanism.
- Do not turn “90% returned to healthy condition” into “the reef recovered.” Colony health, coral cover, and structural recovery are different endpoints; the three-year southern Bahia study found no coral-cover rebound.

## Convergência: supported synthesis

Convergência can state:

> Em 2019, um pulso regional de calor acumulado coincidiu com branqueamento disseminado em Abrolhos. Branquear revelou estresse, mas não determinou um único desfecho: muitas colônias recuperaram a cor, enquanto a mortalidade variou entre espécies e lugares. É por isso que observações conectadas — no tempo, entre recifes e entre organismos — importam.

This synthesis follows the evidence without adding a fourth claim. “Coincided with” is deliberately conservative: the observations and thermal record strongly link the event, while the project has chosen not to make an event-attribution claim.

## Final editorial checklist

Before copy is approved, verify that every passage:

- names the evidence as historical and the Expedition as composite;
- keeps the satellite heat record distinct from field observations;
- gives a place, time, organism, and measurement definition for every number;
- distinguishes bleaching from mortality;
- distinguishes a percentage of one taxon from whole-reef cover;
- retains uncertainty (`± SE` or `± SD`) where the source reports it;
- labels hypotheses as hypotheses;
- exposes the exact source from the visitor-facing reader;
- avoids claims of specific-event attribution, fictional measurement, present-day condition, or institutional partnership.
